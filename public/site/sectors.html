12 sectors, 26 regulators and 48 instruments mapped against the DPDP Act, with the documents an auditor would expect to see.

SEBI

Stock broking and capital markets

Stock brokers, depository participants and other SEBI-regulated intermediaries: cyber resilience, client KYC data, outsourcing and grievance rules sit on top of DPDP.

SEBINSE and BSECDSL and NSDLFIU-IND
8instruments12added controls

Laws, circulars and guidelines

  • SEBI Master Circular for Stock Brokers (17 June 2025) checked against a sourceSEBI · Consolidates the circulars on stock brokers. System audits now cover cyber security, cloud, AI use, order management and risk management systems, by auditors from an empanelled list with a limit on repeat appointments. Check for later updates.
  • SEBI Cybersecurity and Cyber Resilience Framework (CSCRF), August 2024, with the framework for stock brokers and depository participants check before useSEBI · Governance, identification, protection, detection, response and recovery requirements for regulated entities, with periodic cyber audits and reporting of incidents to SEBI and the exchange within six hours of noticing them.
  • SEBI direction to report cyber incidents within six hours (stock brokers and depository participants) checked against a sourceSEBI · Stock brokers and depository participants report cyber attacks, threats, incidents and breaches within six hours of noticing them, in line with the CERT-In timelines.
  • Prevention of Money-laundering Act, 2002 and the PML (Maintenance of Records) Rules, 2005 check before useFIU-IND, SEBI · Client identification and transaction records are maintained for the periods the Rules set (generally at least five years), and suspicious transactions are reported. This is a legal retention duty that limits erasure.
  • SEBI KYC (Know Your Client) Registration Agency framework and master circular on KYC check before useSEBI · KYC records are uploaded to and fetched from the KYC Registration Agencies, verified against official documents and kept current.
  • SEBI circular on outsourcing of activities by intermediaries (December 2011) and framework for adoption of cloud services (March 2023) check before useSEBI · The intermediary stays responsible for outsourced activities: due diligence on the service provider, a written agreement, protection of client data, and access for SEBI and auditors. Cloud use needs a defined framework and data location awareness.
  • SEBI SCORES and the Online Dispute Resolution (ODR) framework check before useSEBI · Investor complaints are received and answered through SCORES within the prescribed time, with escalation to the ODR institutions.
  • SEBI master circular on anti-money laundering and countering the financing of terrorism check before useSEBI, FIU-IND · Client due diligence, ongoing monitoring and suspicious transaction reporting, supported by an AML policy approved by the board.

How it sits with DPDP

  • DPDP s.8(7) lets a Data Fiduciary keep personal data when a law requires it. PMLA and SEBI record-keeping periods therefore override a client's erasure request for those records. The retention schedule must show which law sets each period.
  • DPDP Rule 7 breach intimation to the Board and affected people is in addition to reporting to SEBI, the exchange and CERT-In. One incident can need all four reports.
  • Client KYC and trading data is financial data held at scale. Check whether the Government has designated the firm a Significant Data Fiduciary.
  • Marketing calls and messages to clients also fall under the TRAI consent and preference rules (control CYB-03).

Documents an auditor would expect

  • Board-approved cyber security and resilience policy
  • CISO appointment letter
  • Latest system audit and cyber audit report with closure status
  • Incident response plan naming SEBI, exchange and CERT-In reporting steps
  • Client onboarding KYC form and KRA upload log
  • Outsourcing agreement with cloud or back-office vendor
  • Retention schedule showing legal basis per record type
  • SCORES complaint register and ageing report
  • Disaster recovery drill report
  • Authorised person agreement
RBI

Banking, NBFC and payments

Banks, non-bank lenders and payment firms regulated by the RBI: IT governance, outsourcing, KYC, payment data and digital lending rules sit on top of DPDP.

RBINPCIFIU-IND
6instruments6added controls

Laws, circulars and guidelines

  • RBI Master Direction on Information Technology Governance, Risk, Controls and Assurance Practices (2023) check before useRBI · Board-level IT governance, a named head of information security, risk and control practices, and independent assurance over IT systems.
  • RBI Master Direction on Outsourcing of Information Technology Services (2023) check before useRBI · Due diligence, a written agreement, regulator and auditor access, data protection and exit plans for IT outsourcing and cloud.
  • RBI Master Direction on Know Your Customer (KYC), 2016, as amended check before useRBI · Customer identification, periodic KYC updating and record keeping for the periods the Direction sets.
  • RBI directive on storage of payment system data (April 2018) check before useRBI · Payment system data is stored only in India, with a limited exception for the foreign leg of a cross-border transaction.
  • RBI Digital Lending Directions (2025) and earlier guidelines check before useRBI · Borrower data collection is need-based with prior consent, data is stored on servers in India, and lending apps disclose what they collect and why.
  • RBI cyber security framework and incident reporting directions check before useRBI · A cyber security policy, a security operations capability and prompt reporting of cyber incidents to the RBI.

How it sits with DPDP

  • KYC and transaction records kept under the RBI and PMLA limit a customer's erasure right (DPDP s.8(7)). The retention schedule must show the legal period for each record.
  • The RBI payment data rule is stricter than DPDP s.16 cross-border transfer (which allows transfers except to restricted countries). Where both apply, follow the stricter one.
  • Lenders collecting contacts, location or files through apps should check consent against DPDP s.6 and the RBI digital lending directions together.
  • Banks and large lenders are strong candidates for Significant Data Fiduciary status.

Documents an auditor would expect

  • Board-approved IT and information security policy
  • IT outsourcing agreement and exit plan
  • KYC policy and re-KYC schedule
  • Data localisation attestation for payment data
  • Digital lending app consent screens and privacy notice
  • Cyber incident register with RBI reports
IRDAI

Insurance

Insurers, brokers and third-party administrators regulated by IRDAI: cyber resilience, policyholder data, outsourcing and claims records on top of DPDP.

IRDAI
4instruments5added controls

Laws, circulars and guidelines

  • IRDAI Information and Cyber Security Guidelines (2023) check before useIRDAI · Governance, risk assessment, security controls, incident reporting and audits for the information and cyber security of insurers and intermediaries.
  • IRDAI (Protection of Policyholders' Interests, Operations and Allied Matters of Insurers) Regulations, 2024 check before useIRDAI · Policyholder communication, claims handling and the safeguarding of policyholder information.
  • IRDAI outsourcing guidelines for insurers check before useIRDAI · Due diligence on outsourced partners, written agreements, confidentiality and access for the regulator.
  • IRDAI record-keeping and electronic policy requirements check before useIRDAI · Policy, premium and claims records are maintained for the periods the regulations set, including in electronic form.

How it sits with DPDP

  • Insurers hold health and financial data at scale and share it widely with TPAs, hospitals and reinsurers. Each recipient is a Data Processor or Data Fiduciary and needs a contract.
  • Regulatory retention for policy and claims records limits erasure (DPDP s.8(7)).
  • Marketing to prospects falls under both the DPDP consent rules and TRAI preference rules (control CYB-03).

Documents an auditor would expect

  • Board-approved cyber security policy
  • TPA and hospital data sharing agreements
  • Claims data access matrix
  • Records retention schedule
  • IRDAI cyber audit report
Ministry of Health

Healthcare and health technology

Hospitals, clinics, labs, telemedicine and health apps: patient confidentiality, health records and the ABDM digital health framework on top of DPDP.

Ministry of HealthNational Health Authority (ABDM)NMC
4instruments5added controls

Laws, circulars and guidelines

  • Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission Health Data Management Policy check before useNational Health Authority · Consent-based sharing of health records through the ABDM, with consent artefacts, purpose limits and security practices for participating entities.
  • Telemedicine Practice Guidelines (2020) check before useNMC, Ministry of Health · Patient identification, consent, record keeping and confidentiality for teleconsultations.
  • Clinical Establishments (Registration and Regulation) Act and Rules check before useState authorities · Clinical establishments maintain patient records and make them available to patients on request.
  • Mental Healthcare Act, 2017 and HIV and AIDS (Prevention and Control) Act, 2017: confidentiality check before useMinistry of Health · Special confidentiality for mental health and HIV records, with narrow grounds for disclosure.

How it sits with DPDP

  • DPDP Rule 12 and the Fourth Schedule exempt clinical establishments, healthcare professionals and allied health services from the parental consent requirement for children, but only for health services to the child and only as far as needed to protect the child's health.
  • Health data is among the most sensitive personal data. Look closely at whether a firm could be notified as a Significant Data Fiduciary.
  • Medical record retention periods under clinical rules limit erasure (DPDP s.8(7)).

Documents an auditor would expect

  • Patient consent form
  • Role-based access matrix for the hospital information system
  • Telemedicine consent record
  • Records retention policy
  • Lab and pharmacy data sharing agreement
DoT

Telecom and internet services

Telecom licensees, ISPs and messaging platforms: subscriber verification, cyber security rules and commercial communication rules on top of DPDP.

DoTTRAI
4instruments4added controls

Laws, circulars and guidelines

  • Telecommunications Act, 2023 check before useDoT · Authorisation for telecom services, identification of users, and duties on authorised entities. Rules are being notified in phases, so check which provisions are in force.
  • Telecommunications (Telecom Cyber Security) Rules, 2024 check before useDoT · Telecom entities adopt a cyber security policy, report security incidents within the stated time, and cooperate with the government on security.
  • TRAI Telecom Commercial Communications Customer Preference Regulations, 2018 check before useTRAI · Registered headers and templates, customer preferences and consent for commercial messages and calls.
  • DoT subscriber verification (KYC) instructions check before useDoT · Subscriber identity is verified at enrolment and records are kept for the prescribed period.

How it sits with DPDP

  • DPDP applies to subscriber and usage data alongside the telecom licence conditions. Where a licence requires longer retention, DPDP s.8(7) allows it.
  • Lawful interception and information requests sit within the State exemptions of DPDP s.17(2). Keep a record of every request received.

Documents an auditor would expect

  • Telecom cyber security policy
  • CTSO appointment
  • Subscriber KYC process note
  • Data retention schedule for CDRs
  • DLT registration and consent register
Department of Consumer Affairs

E-commerce and retail

Online marketplaces, retailers and consumer apps: consumer protection rules, dark patterns, payment data and the DPDP erasure schedule for e-commerce.

Department of Consumer AffairsCCPARBI
4instruments5added controls

Laws, circulars and guidelines

  • Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules, 2020 check before useDepartment of Consumer Affairs · E-commerce entities disclose seller and product information, set up grievance redressal with a named officer, and protect consumer data.
  • Guidelines for Prevention and Regulation of Dark Patterns, 2023 check before useCCPA · Interfaces must not trick users, for example through false urgency, forced actions, hidden costs or drip pricing. Consent flows need a check against the listed patterns.
  • RBI card-on-file tokenisation rules check before useRBI · Merchants do not store actual card numbers. Tokens are used instead.
  • DPDP Rules, Third Schedule: retention for e-commerce, gaming and social media (Rule 8(3)) checked against a sourceMeitY · Large e-commerce entities, online gaming intermediaries and social media intermediaries above stated user thresholds erase personal data after three years of the user's last interaction, after giving at least 48 hours' notice. Check the Schedule for the thresholds.

How it sits with DPDP

  • Consumer-facing platforms depend heavily on consent. A consent flow that fails the dark patterns guidelines may also fail the DPDP test of free, specific and unambiguous consent.
  • Large platforms may also be notified as Significant Data Fiduciaries.

Documents an auditor would expect

  • Sign-up and checkout consent screenshots
  • Grievance officer page
  • Payment gateway tokenisation attestation
  • Inactivity erasure job configuration
  • Marketing consent register
MeitY

Streaming, publishing, social media and online gaming: viewer profiling, children's access controls and platform rules on top of DPDP.

MeitYMinistry of Information and Broadcasting
2instruments4added controls

Laws, circulars and guidelines

  • Information Technology (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules, 2021 check before useMeitY, MIB · Grievance redressal, content classification and age ratings, parental locks and access control for content rated for older audiences.
  • DPDP Rules, Third Schedule: retention for social media and online gaming checked against a sourceMeitY · Social media intermediaries and online gaming intermediaries above stated user thresholds erase data after three years of inactivity, with 48 hours' advance notice.

DGCA

DGCABCASMinistry of Civil Aviation
3instruments4added controls

Laws, circulars and guidelines

  • Passenger Name Record Information Regulations, 2022 check before useMinistry of Civil Aviation · Airlines provide passenger name record data to the Passenger Information Unit and protect it as the regulations require.
  • DigiYatra biometric boarding framework check before useMinistry of Civil Aviation · Consent-based facial recognition for boarding, with passenger data kept in the passenger's own wallet and deleted after the journey as the policy specifies.

MeitY

AI and data analytics

Organisations that build or use AI, profiling and analytics: lawful training data, algorithmic due diligence and responsible AI guidance on top of DPDP.

MeitY
3instruments5added controls

Laws, circulars and guidelines

  • India AI Governance Guidelines (MeitY, November 2025) check before useMeitY · Principle-based guidance for responsible AI: accountability, transparency, safety and fairness. Voluntary guidance, not a binding law, so describe it that way.
  • MeitY advisories on deepfakes and AI-generated content check before useMeitY · Intermediaries are asked to detect and act on synthetic content and to label AI-generated material.
  • DPDP Rule 13 (Significant Data Fiduciary due diligence on algorithmic software) checked against a sourceMeitY · A Significant Data Fiduciary verifies that algorithmic software it uses for personal data does not risk the rights of Data Principals.

How it sits with DPDP

  • DPDP s.3(c)(ii) excludes personal data that is made publicly available by the Data Principal or under a legal duty to publish. It does not cover everything on the internet, so scraped training data needs a careful lawful-ground review.
  • Automated profiling of individuals strengthens the case for Significant Data Fiduciary treatment.

Documents an auditor would expect

  • AI system register
  • Training data source and consent record
  • Model risk or bias assessment
  • Human review procedure
  • AI governance policy
Ministry of Education

Education and edtech

Schools, colleges and edtech platforms: most users are children, so parental consent and the ban on tracking children come first.

Ministry of EducationState education departmentsUGC
2instruments4added controls

Laws, circulars and guidelines

  • DPDP Act s.9 and Rules 10 to 12 (children's data), applied to education checked against a sourceMeitY · Verifiable parental consent before processing a child's data; no tracking, behavioural monitoring or targeted ads aimed at children. Rule 12 and the Fourth Schedule exempt educational institutions only as far as tracking and monitoring are needed for educational activities or the safety of enrolled children.
  • Education records and examination body rules check before useState boards, UGC · Student and examination records are kept for the periods the board or university requires.

How it sits with DPDP

  • Almost every student is a child under 18, so s.9 applies by default. The fine for failing the children's duties can reach ₹200 crore.
  • The class-wide Fourth Schedule exemption is narrow. Do not assume it covers marketing or analytics.

Documents an auditor would expect

  • Admission form with parental consent
  • Learning app privacy notice
  • Vendor agreements for learning tools
  • Student records retention schedule
Data Protection Board of India

MSMEs and small businesses

Small and micro businesses: the Act has no size exemption, so the aim is a proportionate set of essentials a small firm can actually do.

Data Protection Board of IndiaMSME Ministry (support schemes)
1instrument4added controls

Laws, circulars and guidelines

  • DPDP Act, 2023: application to small businesses checked against a sourceMeitY · The Act applies by what an organisation does with personal data, not by its size. The Government can exempt classes of Data Fiduciaries, including startups, from some duties (s.17(3)). Check the latest notification before telling a client they are exempt.

How it sits with DPDP

  • The penalty ceilings in the Schedule are maximums, and the Board looks at how serious and repeated a failure is and whether it was put right. A small firm that acts in good faith is in a different position from one that ignores the rules.
  • A startup exemption under s.17(3) needs a Government notification. Until you have confirmed one covers the client, assume the Act applies in full.

Documents an auditor would expect

  • Customer data list
  • Simple privacy notice
  • Backup and sign-in settings screenshot
  • Named privacy contact
  • Supplier list with agreements
MeitY

Government and public bodies

Departments, public sector undertakings and instrumentalities: the State has its own exemptions and legitimate uses, but duties on security and accuracy remain.

MeitYCERT-InNIC
4instruments5added controls

Laws, circulars and guidelines

  • DPDP Act s.7 (certain legitimate uses), s.17(2) (State exemptions) and Rule 5 checked against a sourceMeitY · The State and its instrumentalities may process data without consent for a subsidy, benefit, service, licence or permit when the data principal has already given it or it is in State records, subject to standards under Rule 5 and the Second Schedule. Notified agencies can be exempted for sovereignty, security and public order (s.17(2)(a)).
  • Right to Information Act, 2005, s.8(1)(j), as amended by the DPDP Act check before useCentral Information Commission · The personal information exemption under RTI was widened by DPDP. Public authorities should check how the amended section affects disclosures.
  • Aadhaar Act, 2016 and Aadhaar (Authentication) Regulations check before useUIDAI · Aadhaar numbers are collected, stored and used only under the Act, with consent for authentication and secure storage in the vault.
  • CERT-In guidelines for government entities and MeitY cloud (MeghRaj) policy check before useCERT-In, MeitY · Government entities follow CERT-In security guidelines and use empanelled cloud services.

How it sits with DPDP

  • Many government services can process data without consent under s.7. Security safeguards, breach intimation and accuracy duties still apply unless the entity is specifically exempted under s.17(2).
  • State exemptions under s.17(2)(a) need a Government notification for the particular agency. Do not assume one applies.
  • Government bodies holding citizen data at scale are strong candidates for Significant Data Fiduciary status.

Documents an auditor would expect

  • Service-wise legal basis register
  • Aadhaar vault configuration
  • Cloud empanelment evidence
  • RTI personal data review procedure
  • Citizen grievance procedure
