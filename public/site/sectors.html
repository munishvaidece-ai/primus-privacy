SEBI

Stock broking and capital markets

Stock brokers, depository participants and other SEBI-regulated intermediaries: cyber resilience, client KYC data, outsourcing and grievance rules sit on top of DPDP.

SEBINSE and BSECDSL and NSDLFIU-IND
8instruments12added controls

Laws, circulars and guidelines

  • SEBI Master Circular for Stock Brokers (17 June 2025) checked against a sourceSEBI · Consolidates the circulars on stock brokers. System audits now cover cyber security, cloud, AI use, order management and risk management systems, by auditors from an empanelled list with a limit on repeat appointments. Check for later updates.
  • SEBI Cybersecurity and Cyber Resilience Framework (CSCRF), August 2024, with the framework for stock brokers and depository participants check before useSEBI · Governance, identification, protection, detection, response and recovery requirements for regulated entities, with periodic cyber audits and reporting of incidents to SEBI and the exchange within six hours of noticing them.
  • SEBI direction to report cyber incidents within six hours (stock brokers and depository participants) checked against a sourceSEBI · Stock brokers and depository participants report cyber attacks, threats, incidents and breaches within six hours of noticing them, in line with the CERT-In timelines.
  • Prevention of Money-laundering Act, 2002 and the PML (Maintenance of Records) Rules, 2005 check before useFIU-IND, SEBI · Client identification and transaction records are maintained for the periods the Rules set (generally at least five years), and suspicious transactions are reported. This is a legal retention duty that limits erasure.
  • SEBI KYC (Know Your Client) Registration Agency framework and master circular on KYC check before useSEBI · KYC records are uploaded to and fetched from the KYC Registration Agencies, verified against official documents and kept current.
  • SEBI circular on outsourcing of activities by intermediaries (December 2011) and framework for adoption of cloud services (March 2023) check before useSEBI · The intermediary stays responsible for outsourced activities: due diligence on the service provider, a written agreement, protection of client data, and access for SEBI and auditors. Cloud use needs a defined framework and data location awareness.
  • SEBI SCORES and the Online Dispute Resolution (ODR) framework check before useSEBI · Investor complaints are received and answered through SCORES within the prescribed time, with escalation to the ODR institutions.
  • SEBI master circular on anti-money laundering and countering the financing of terrorism check before useSEBI, FIU-IND · Client due diligence, ongoing monitoring and suspicious transaction reporting, supported by an AML policy approved by the board.

How it sits with DPDP

  • DPDP s.8(7) lets a Data Fiduciary keep personal data when a law requires it. PMLA and SEBI record-keeping periods therefore override a client's erasure request for those records. The retention schedule must show which law sets each period.
  • DPDP Rule 7 breach intimation to the Board and affected people is in addition to reporting to SEBI, the exchange and CERT-In. One incident can need all four reports.
  • Client KYC and trading data is financial data held at scale. Check whether the Government has designated the firm a Significant Data Fiduciary.
  • Marketing calls and messages to clients also fall under the TRAI consent and preference rules (control CYB-03).

Documents an auditor would expect

  • Board-approved cyber security and resilience policy
  • CISO appointment letter
  • Latest system audit and cyber audit report with closure status
  • Incident response plan naming SEBI, exchange and CERT-In reporting steps
  • Client onboarding KYC form and KRA upload log
  • Outsourcing agreement with cloud or back-office vendor
  • Retention schedule showing legal basis per record type
  • SCORES complaint register and ageing report
  • Disaster recovery drill report
  • Authorised person agreement
RBI

Banking, NBFC and payments

Banks, non-bank lenders and payment firms regulated by the RBI: IT governance, outsourcing, KYC, payment data and digital lending rules sit on top of DPDP.

RBINPCIFIU-IND
6instruments6added controls

Laws, circulars and guidelines

  • RBI Master Direction on Information Technology Governance, Risk, Controls and Assurance Practices (2023) check before useRBI · Board-level IT governance, a named head of information security, risk and control practices, and independent assurance over IT systems.
  • RBI Master Direction on Outsourcing of Information Technology Services (2023) check before useRBI · Due diligence, a written agreement, regulator and auditor access, data protection and exit plans for IT outsourcing and cloud.
  • RBI Master Direction on Know Your Customer (KYC), 2016, as amended check before useRBI · Customer identification, periodic KYC updating and record keeping for the periods the Direction sets.
  • RBI directive on storage of payment system data (April 2018) check before useRBI · Payment system data is stored only in India, with a limited exception for the foreign leg of a cross-border transaction.
  • RBI Digital Lending Directions (2025) and earlier guidelines check before useRBI · Borrower data collection is need-based with prior consent, data is stored on servers in India, and lending apps disclose what they collect and why.
  • RBI cyber security framework and incident reporting directions check before useRBI · A cyber security policy, a security operations capability and prompt reporting of cyber incidents to the RBI.

How it sits with DPDP

  • KYC and transaction records kept under the RBI and PMLA limit a customer's erasure right (DPDP s.8(7)). The retention schedule must show the legal period for each record.
  • The RBI payment data rule is stricter than DPDP s.16 cross-border transfer (which allows transfers except to restricted countries). Where both apply, follow the stricter one.
  • Lenders collecting contacts, location or files through apps should check consent against DPDP s.6 and the RBI digital lending directions together.
  • Banks and large lenders are strong candidates for Significant Data Fiduciary status.

Documents an auditor would expect

  • Board-approved IT and information security policy
  • IT outsourcing agreement and exit plan
  • KYC policy and re-KYC schedule
  • Data localisation attestation for payment data
  • Digital lending app consent screens and privacy notice
  • Cyber incident register with RBI reports

CDSCO

Pharmaceuticals and life sciences

Drug makers, CROs, clinical-trial sponsors and pharma marketers: trial participants, adverse-event reports and prescriber data on top of DPDP. Hospitals and clinics are under Healthcare.

CDSCODCGIICMRNPPAState drug controllers
5instruments6added controls

Laws, circulars and guidelines

  • New Drugs and Clinical Trials Rules, 2019 check before useCDSCO · Informed consent, ethics committee oversight, confidentiality of participant identity, serious adverse event reporting and record keeping for clinical trials and bioavailability studies.
  • ICMR National Ethical Guidelines for Biomedical and Health Research Involving Human Participants (2017) check before useICMR · Informed consent, privacy and confidentiality of participants, secondary use of data and biological samples, and data sharing with sponsors and researchers.
  • Pharmacovigilance Programme of India (PvPI) adverse drug reaction reporting check before useIPC, CDSCO · Marketing authorisation holders collect, assess and report adverse drug reactions, which carry patient and reporter details.
  • Uniform Code for Pharmaceutical Marketing Practices check before useDepartment of Pharmaceuticals · Conduct rules for contact with healthcare professionals, including records of payments and engagements that name individual practitioners.
  • DPDP Act s.17(2)(b) (research, archiving and statistical purposes) and Rule 16 checked against a sourceMeitY · Processing for research, archiving or statistics is exempt from most duties only if the data is not used to take decisions about a person and the standards in the Second Schedule are followed.

How it sits with DPDP

  • Trial participants' health data is among the most sensitive personal data. Consider whether the organisation may be notified as a Significant Data Fiduciary.
  • Clinical trial rules require records to be kept for fixed periods, which limits erasure under DPDP s.8(7).
  • The research exemption in s.17(2)(b) is narrow. It does not cover marketing or decisions about a person.
  • Coded (pseudonymised) trial data is still personal data when the sponsor or CRO can link the code back to the person.

Documents an auditor would expect

  • Informed consent form and participant information sheet
  • Trial data-sharing agreement with sponsor or CRO
  • Pharmacovigilance SOP and safety database access list
  • Records retention schedule
  • Healthcare professional engagement register

MeitY

IT services, BPO and global capability centres

Software and IT services firms, outsourcing companies and GCCs: mostly Data Processors for clients abroad, with real exposure on their own staff, candidates and Indian clients.

MeitYSTPI and SEZ authoritiesClient regulators abroad (by contract)
3instruments6added controls

Laws, circulars and guidelines

  • DPDP Act s.17(1)(d) and s.8(2): processing for clients outside India, and Data Processors checked against a sourceMeitY · Most duties do not apply to personal data of people outside India that is processed in India under a contract with a person outside India.   • Clients' foreign-law terms still bind the firm by contract even where DPDP is lighter.

  • Sample data-processing agreement and sub-processor list

Data Protection Board of India

MSMEs and small businesses

Small and micro businesses. If you are a small business, also choose your industry sector, because sector rules still apply to you. The Act has no size exemption, so the aim is a proportionate set of essentials a small firm can actually do.

Data Protection Board of IndiaMSME Ministry (support schemes)
1instrument6added controls

Laws, circulars and guidelines

  • DPDP Act, 2023: application to small businesses checked against a sourceMeitY · The Act applies by what an organisation does with personal data, not by its size. The Government can exempt classes of Data Fiduciaries, including startups, from some duties (s.17(3)). Check the latest notification before telling a client they are exempt.

How it sits with DPDP

  • The penalty ceilings in the Schedule are maximums, and the Board looks at how serious and repeated a failure is and whether it was put right. A small firm that acts in good faith is in a different position from one that ignores the rules.
  • A startup exemption under s.17(3) needs a Government notification. Until you have confirmed one covers the client, assume the Act applies in full.

Documents an auditor would expect

  • Customer data list
  • Simple privacy notice
  • Backup and sign-in settings screenshot
  • Named privacy contact
  • Supplier list with agreements

How it sits with DPDP

  • A PSU is a company or body owned or controlled by government. It is not automatically the State for DPDP purposes. Treat the State's legitimate uses (s.7) and exemptions (s.17(2)) as available only after a legal view and, for exemptions, a notification.
  • PSUs with large customer or citizen bases are strong candidates for Significant Data Fiduciary status.
  • Public sector banks and insurers are assessed under Banking or Insurance as well as this sector. Select both and the controls will add up without repeating.
  • Duties on security safeguards, breach intimation and accuracy apply even where the State's legitimate uses are used.
